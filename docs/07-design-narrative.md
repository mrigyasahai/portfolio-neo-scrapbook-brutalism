# Design Narrative — Story, Emotion, Hierarchy, Rhythm

This document is the *why* behind every section and component. It answers: what does the visitor feel, and in what order?

## 1. The Story

A portfolio is a story with a single thesis:

> **"I solve product problems, and I build the products that fix them."**

The page is structured as a classic narrative arc — **exposition → philosophy → proof → depth → continuity → person → invitation** — each section one beat, no filler.

| Beat | Section | Story function |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposition** | Hero | Who I am, what I claim, why you should keep scrolling |
| **Terrain** | Marquee | The breadth of domains I operate in (fast skim) |
| **Philosophy** | How I Work | The belief system behind the work (do we think alike?) |
| **Proof** | Work | The receipts — shipped products with numbers |
| **Depth** | Writing | Intellectual rigor + documentation craft |
| **Continuity** | Timeline | I've built for years, and I'm still accelerating |
| **Person** | About | The human behind the work |
| **Invitation** | Contact | The single, unmissable next step |

## 2. Emotional Flow

Designing for Norman's three levels — visceral (instinct), behavioral (use), reflective (meaning) — in sequence:

1. **Visceral (0–50ms):** warm cream paper, thick black strokes, saturated pastels, rotated stickers. Instantly reads *handmade, warm, confident, non-corporate.* The anti-template signal happens before a single word is read.
2. **Behavioral (seconds):** one-bordered nav, sticker-press buttons, outcome-first cards, consistent CTA placement. Effortless scanning + obvious actions = "this is easy to use."
3. **Reflective (after the scroll):** "This person cares about craft, can design *and* build, and has taste." This is the layer that makes a design engineer or product leader *remember* the person.

### Emotional arc (per section)

```
intrigue → recognition → alignment → conviction → trust → momentum → connection → action
  Hero      Marquee      Method       Work       Writing    Timeline     About      Contact
```

- **Hero:** curiosity + relief ("finally not a template portfolio").
- **Marquee:** momentum; breadth without a word of copy.
- **Method:** alignment — founders/leaders feel "this is how I'd want them to work."
- **Work:** conviction — the page stops claiming and starts proving.
- **Writing:** trust/respect — rare combination of builder + communicator.
- **Timeline:** confidence — consistency across years, still climbing.
- **About:** connection — a person, not a profile.
- **Contact:** opportunity — the door is open and one tap away.

## 3. Information Hierarchy

Every section has **exactly one job**, and nothing on the page competes with it.

```
PRIMARY   · Claim (Hero) + Proof (Work)      → answers "is this person good?"
SECONDARY · Method (How I Work) + Depth (Writing) → answers "how do they think & communicate?"
TERTIARY  · Continuity (Timeline) + Person (About) → answers "who is this, and is it consistent?"
ACTION    · Contact                            → answers "how do I reach them?"
```

**In-section hierarchy (the universal recipe):**
1. Section header (tab + claim)
2. The one highlighted phrase (color emphasis)
3. Numbered/grouped content cards (evidence)
4. One supporting voice element (margin note)
5. One CTA (bottom-left)

**Per-card hierarchy:** outcome/number → problem → move → tags. Numbers first, always.

## 4. First Impression (the 5-second test)

What must be readable inside the **first viewport, zero scrolling**:

1. Name + roles (who)
2. The claim with its highlighter block (what + the one idea)
3. Three hard numbers (proof it's not a claim)
4. A visible CTA (what to do next)
5. Wordmark + nav (how to move around)

Ordering by visual weight: claim (largest) → proof strip → CTA → name/nav (chrome). If a recruiter reads only one viewport, they should still know who this is, what they do, and that there are numbers behind it.

## 5. Visual Rhythm

- **Chapter bands:** cream → pink → cream → mint → lavender → blue → yellow, each a full-bleed "spread." Rhythm = alternating pulse between warm neutral and saturated accent.
- **Repetition with discipline:** the card recipe repeats every section (recognition), while colors rotate (novelty). Numbered indices repeat; highlights are used **at most twice per viewport**.
- **Pace contrast:** dense factual zones (Work 2×2 grid, Writing matrix) sit between open narrative zones (Hero, Timeline). Density → relief → density → relief.
- **Punctuation:** the black marquee band is the metronome separating act one (who/how) from act two (what/evidence).
- **Consistent CTA position:** bottom-left of content blocks — once learned, the eye returns (Jakob's Law).

## 6. Recruiter Psychology

- **Skim time is 30–90 seconds.** Numbers first, verbs over adjectives: `TAT −63%` beats "dramatically improved."
- **They scan for fit, not flavor:** role fit (all 5 hats visible), proof (numbers), signal of taste (the design itself is a portfolio sample), contactability (visible email).
- **Primacy + recency:** decisions anchor on the first viewport (Hero) and last (Contact). Both are the strongest moments.
- **Specificity beats superlatives:** each metric is tied to a hat (`[AI]`, `[PRODUCT]`, `[SHOPIFY]`) so the proof maps to a role.
- **Trust via craft:** a handmade, systematic design says "this person ships with care" — before they read a single case study.

## 7. Product Storytelling (the case-study formula)

Every case study follows the same three-beat arc, outcome-first for scanning:

```
PROBLEM  → the tension (one line, no jargon)
MOVE     → what I built and the key decision/tradeoff (product thinking made visible)
OUTCOME  → the receipt (monospace metric, tied to a hat)
```

- The **receipt** leads (scanability). The **move** is where product thinking is proven (founders read this). The **margin note** is the "director's commentary" — the opinionated human line that a résumé can't say.
- Six stories = one story: *from first build, through Shopify commerce, to AI agents and documentation systems.* A coherent arc, not a random grid.

## 8. What Every Section Must NOT Do

- Hero: not a generic "Hello, I'm..." — no stock-photo handshake, no wordcloud of tools.
- Work: not a screenshot grid — every tile must contain a number and a decision.
- Writing: not a blog drop-down — a matrix proving documentation craft.
- Contact: not a form wall — one CTA and three tag links.
- No section sells anything; every section moves the reader toward *contact*.
